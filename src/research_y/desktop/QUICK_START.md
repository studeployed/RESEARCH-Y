## Create or open a project

Open **RESEARCH-Y** from the Windows Start menu. To start a project, type its name, click **Create project in folder**, and choose the folder where you want it saved. RESEARCH-Y creates a new folder inside it and shows the project name. To return later, click **Open project folder** and choose the folder containing `project.ryproj`. To open a different project while working, click **Switch project**.

## Set up your first search

1. On **Plan search**, enter your **Research theme** under **Research question**. For example, enter *RV Failure*. Under **Research objectives (optional)** you could write *Find studies on right ventricular failure in adults.* Enter your name in **Approving researcher’s name**, then click **Save question**.
   Look for **✓ Saved** beside **Edit question**. Your question and researcher name are stored in this project and restored when you reopen it. To change them, click **Edit question**, make your changes and save again.
2. Follow **Next** in **Steps to your search**. Enter a **Group name**, such as *Right ventricular failure*, and click **Add group**. A group keeps related search terms together.
3. Inside the group, enter *right ventricular failure* under **Search term or phrase** and press **Enter** or click **Add term**. After it is added, the empty field is ready for the next phrase. Repeat with *right heart failure* if you want to search for that phrase too. Click a saved term to edit it or ask for suggestions.
4. Click **Preview search** and read the wording for each database. An article can match **any** enabled term across the groups. The example objective alone does not limit results to adults. Use **Database selection and optional search limits** to review the available limits.
5. To change the wording, click **Edit search plan**. When the preview matches your intent, click **Approve and save search**. This saves the search plan. The databases are queried in a later step.

Your unfinished groups and terms are saved in the project as you add or edit them. After approval, the saved terms remain visible. Choose **Edit a copy** to revise them without changing the earlier search, or **Start a blank search** for a different plan. You do not need to retype a saved search after reopening the project.

## Run the search and use the results

1. On **Run search**, open the saved search you want, then click **Check saved plan**. A dedicated review screen shows the saved question, phrases, limits and database coverage beside **Search checks**. Choose **Checked**, **Needs changes** or **Not checked yet** for each item. Hover over an item for help. **Database checks** shows what the app has verified and any actual pilot results; **Not tested yet** means a provider response is still missing. Checking that item records your review of the evidence, not proof of an untested query. Under **Reviewer details**, **I am reviewing my own search** uses your saved name; clear it to name another reviewer. Click **Review answers**, then **Confirm and save checks**. **Back to Run search** closes the screen. Unfinished answers remain available through **Review search** while this app window stays open.
2. Under **Collect your search results**, click **Show collection steps** if needed, then **Continue PubMed** or **Continue Web of Science**. This opens the next step for that database. Read its preview and confirm the work to send requests. For an optional count before collection, open **Try a PubMed search**, preview a group, then click **Confirm one PubMed count-only request**. Web of Science has its own pilot. Pilot counts are estimates of matches, not collected articles.
3. Keep the app open during collection. The database cards show saved identifiers and article details; **Continue** opens the next unfinished step. Pause controls appear for a running session. For PubMed, identifiers come first and article details follow. A paused or interrupted search retains saved progress. Advanced collection and recovery tools remain available when a request needs investigation.
4. Open **Results** to see articles immediately. Search or filter the list, then click **Open** on an article to read its details. Tick a row to select it; there is no separate Apply button. Selections on other pages are kept. **Deselect all matching** clears matching selections. Refresh results after more records have been collected.
5. On **Export**, choose articles, a search strategy workbook or a reviewed master workbook. For articles, choose all, filtered or selected rows. Use `.xlsx` for Excel or `.ris` for a reference manager. Abstract text requires an explicit permission declaration. Enter a filename and click **Choose folder and save**. Wait for **Saved and verified**, then use **Open file** or **Open folder**. Existing files are never overwritten. An interrupted report can be reopened under **Previous report exports** and continued or verified. Full identifiers and specialist recovery tools are available in advanced sections and **Project details**.

## Reading the screen

The project heading, navigation and next step stay visible as you scroll. Select output text with your mouse and use **Ctrl+C** to copy it. Hover over an abbreviation such as PRESS, DOI or API for its meaning. Quiet grey panels contain routine guidance, amber panels flag cautions, red panels show errors or blocked actions, and blue panels identify results. The wording in each panel explains its status.

PRESS means Peer Review of Electronic Search Strategies. Search checks adapts that guidance into questions about your research question, alternative terms, search logic, database syntax, spelling, fields and limits. Your answers record what you checked and what still needs attention. You can also record an external review when another person has reviewed the strategy.
