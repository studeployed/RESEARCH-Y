## Create or open a project

Open **RESEARCH-Y** from the Windows Start menu. To start a project, type its name, click **Create project in folder**, and choose the folder where you want it saved. RESEARCH-Y creates a new folder inside it and shows the project name. To return later, click **Open project folder** and choose the folder containing `project.ryproj`. To open a different project while working, click **Switch project**.

## Set up your first search

1. On **Plan search**, enter your **Research theme** under **Research question**. For example, enter *RV Failure*. Under **Research objectives (optional)** you could write *Find studies on right ventricular failure in adults.* Enter your name in **Approving researcher’s name**, then click **Save question**.
2. Enter a **Group name**, such as *Right ventricular failure*, and click **Add group**. A group keeps related search terms together.
3. Inside the group, enter *right ventricular failure* under **Search term or phrase** and click **Add term**. Repeat with *right heart failure* if you want to search for that phrase too. Click a saved term to edit it or ask for suggestions.
4. Click **Preview search** and read the wording for each database. An article can match **any** enabled term across the groups. The example objective alone does not limit results to adults. Use **Database selection and optional search limits** to review the available limits.
5. To change the wording, click **Edit search plan**. When the preview matches your intent, click **Approve and save search**. This saves the search plan. The databases are queried in a later step.

## Run the search and use the results

1. On **Run search**, open the saved search you want, then click **Check saved plan**. Review the checklist answers, lock them and confirm the checklist when ready.
2. Open **Try a PubMed search** to preview a pilot for one group. Click **Confirm one PubMed count-only request** to get a match count. Web of Science has its own pilot. To retrieve records, open **Collect and verify records**, choose a source and confirm the work shown in the preview.
3. Read the run progress to see which records have been collected. For PubMed, identifier retrieval finds the records and metadata retrieval brings in their details. Review the publications and any proposed matches before preparing a results workbook.
4. Use **Results** to review records. On **Export**, open **Strategy and reviewed workbooks** and choose **Prepare strategy workbook** for the search plan or **Prepare reviewed master workbook** for reviewed publication results. Save the file, then click **Verify workbook**. Full search identifiers, audit history and the project location are under **Project details**.
